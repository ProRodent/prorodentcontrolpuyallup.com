A small former coal-mining town.

Carbonado began as a company town built around coal mining in the late 1800s, and it remains one of the smallest incorporated towns in our coverage area, with a compact residential core surrounded by forest and the Carbon River. That history left the town with an older housing stock, and its small size and forested surroundings shape the kind of rodent activity we see here.

Our coverage includes the full 98323 ZIP, so whether your home sits in the small town core or on a more forested parcel nearby, tell us your address when you call and we will confirm coverage and timing.

What we handle on calls from Carbonado.

Calls from Carbonado describe patterns typical of a small town surrounded by dense forest: rat burrows near a fence line or wooded edge, and mice moving into an older home or outbuilding as the weather turns wet. Dead-rodent odors also come up after an unnoticed problem runs its course for a while. Our rat control service addresses the outdoor, ground-level pattern common near wooded property lines, while mouse control covers the indoor side of the same seasonal shift.

Houses built for a company town, not for today's building code.

The homes that make up Carbonado's small residential core were largely built to house mine workers and their families, not to any standard a modern inspector would recognize. Settling and shifting have had well over a century to open gaps that a newer build simply wouldn't have. We seal what we find with hardware cloth, sheet metal and mortar under our rodent exclusion service, materials chosen specifically because they outlast the foam patches that tend to fail fastest on a structure this age.

Where chewing has already gotten into insulation or wiring before anyone noticed, rodent damage repair picks up once the animals are cleared and the gap that let them in is closed.

The town's edge is the forest's edge.

There's very little distance between the last house in Carbonado and the tree line, and that closeness is really the whole story behind the ground-level rat activity we see here. A shed at the back of a lot, right where the yard gives way to woods, is usually the first place worth checking, and our backyard and outdoor rodent control visit starts exactly there before working back toward the house.

A car left parked at the edge of town.

With forest pressing in close on most sides, a vehicle that sits for even a short stretch near the treeline in Carbonado is at real risk of picking up a nest. Our vehicle and car rodent removal visit checks the engine bay and cabin intake regardless of how often the car actually gets driven.

Getting a technician out to a town this size.

There's no standing crew based in Carbonado itself, since a town this small doesn't support one, but that never changes how quickly or thoroughly a call from here gets handled. A technician routes in from the Puyallup base the same way for every call, and confirming your exact address ahead of time, given the winding roads leading in, helps that visit start on time.

When to call it an emergency.

Active chewing near wiring, a nest close to a furnace, or a burrow undermining a shed or foundation are reasons to say so clearly when you call. The line is open 24/7, and we prioritize those situations against the regular schedule rather than making you wait for the next open slot.
