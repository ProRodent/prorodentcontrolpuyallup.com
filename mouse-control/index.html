Crawl spaces, garages and utility gaps: where mice live in Puyallup homes.

Most homes in and around Puyallup sit over a crawl space or a garage that connects to the living area, and both give mice a quiet, protected route into the house. A vent screen with a torn corner, a garage door threshold with daylight showing under it, or a gap where a dryer vent passes through siding are all common doorways. A mouse that gets into one of these voids can travel to a kitchen or a bedroom wall without ever crossing an open floor.

So we start at the crawl space access and the garage. Pull back any stored boxes near the garage wall and look at the corners where the slab meets the framing. Fresh gnaw marks or dark, greasy rub marks along that edge tell us mice are already using the space as a route into the house.

House mouse or deer mouse.

Two mice show up around homes in the Puyallup area, and the difference changes where we place traps and what we seal. House mice live with people year-round and prefer indoor voids. Deer mice come in from brush, woodpiles and blackberry brambles at the edge of a lot, and they show up more in sheds, garages and crawl spaces than deep inside a house.

If you are unsure which one you have, do not guess from a single glimpse. We look at droppings, rub marks and entry points during the visit and match the plan to the animal.

Why a few mice become dozens.

Indoors, mice breed all year. Gestation is about 19 to 21 days, and a female can have 5 to 10 litters a year, with around 5 or 6 pups in each. That means a pair you hear in October can be a crowded colony by spring, especially in a mild, wet climate where mice rarely have to fight through a hard freeze.

For that reason, a few snap traps from the hardware store rarely finish the job. They remove the mice you catch, but the unsealed gap keeps letting new ones in, and the ones already breeding in the wall or crawl space keep producing. Speed matters, and so does a plan that cuts off the supply.

Treatment plus exclusion: the two halves of mouse control.

Treatment removes the mice that are already inside. Exclusion closes the ways in. Either half alone fails: trapping without sealing turns into a monthly routine, and sealing without trapping locks live mice inside with your food and your wiring.

  • We inspect inside and out, mark every gap of 1/4 inch or more, and note where droppings and rub marks concentrate.
  • We set snap traps and, where the site allows it, tamper-resistant stations in the spots mice actually travel, usually along walls, behind appliances and near the crawl space access.
  • We seal gaps with materials mice cannot chew through, such as hardware cloth, sheet metal, copper mesh and mortar. Steel wool alone does not hold up in a wet climate, so we do not rely on it.
  • We return to check catches, remove traps and confirm that activity has stopped before the work is closed.

The sealing step is its own service when a house has many entry points, and you can read how it works on our rodent exclusion page. If the activity is concentrated in a garage or basement that connects to the living space, our garage and basement rodent control service covers those areas specifically.

Fall rains and mouse activity.

Western Washington's rainy season runs long, and mice start pushing toward shelter well before the first real cold snap most other climates get. That means a Puyallup home can see mouse activity pick up from September through the wet winter months, longer than homeowners moving from a drier region might expect.

If your house has been quiet during the day because everyone is out, check the signs on a weekend morning. Look for droppings along baseboards and in the back of the garage, and listen at dusk when the house is still. Tell us your daily pattern on the call, because it helps us decide when to set traps and when to return.

Do this before we arrive.

  • Store pantry food, pet food and birdseed in hard containers with tight lids.
  • Do not sweep or vacuum droppings. Ventilate the room for 30 minutes, wear gloves, and wet them with disinfectant first.
  • Do not scatter loose poison. It can kill mice inside walls, where the smell lasts for weeks, and it puts pets at risk.
  • Note where you heard or saw activity, and what time of day.
  • Clear access to the crawl space entry or garage corner so we can see the gap.

Rodents do not wait for a convenient time. Reach the line and we will get you on the schedule.

Questions about mouse control

How do I know it is mice and not rats?

Mouse droppings are small and pointed, about 1/8 to 1/4 inch. Rat droppings are closer to 1/2 to 3/4 inch and blunt. Mice also leave small, scattered rub marks along baseboards, while rats tend to leave larger gnaw holes and burrows outside.

Can I just use glue boards or poison?

We do not recommend loose poison in a house with pets or children, and glue boards catch other animals and often leave mice alive for hours. Snap traps and tamper-resistant stations placed on travel routes work better, and sealing the gaps keeps new mice out.

Can I call after hours about mice?

Yes, the line is open around the clock. If mice are in a bedroom or chewing wiring, say so first so we can flag the call as urgent. A same-day visit depends on the schedule.

How long until the mice are gone?

It depends on the number of mice and how many entry points the house has. Small problems can sometimes settle in days, while an established colony in a crawl space can take a few visits. We check catches and activity before closing the work.

Why do mice seem worse once fall rains start?

Cooling, wetter weather from September on pushes mice toward warm, dry shelter, and homes with gaps around utility lines, garage doors and crawl space vents see the first arrivals. Sealing before the rains set in is the best prevention.

Are deer mice a real problem around Puyallup homes?

They can be, especially in sheds, garages and crawl spaces close to woodpiles, brush or blackberry brambles. They nest in stored items and firewood, so we look at outbuildings and cut-back cover as well as the house itself.

My house is empty all day. Does that matter?

It can. Mice tend to be more active in quiet houses, and traps may catch more when nobody is moving around. Tell us your schedule and we will plan the visit and the trap checks around it.
