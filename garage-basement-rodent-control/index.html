Why garages and basements draw activity first.

A garage or basement is usually the part of a house closest to ground level, and it often has more entry points than the main living space: a door threshold, a gap around a garage door opener conduit, a dryer vent, or a foundation crack. Add stored boxes, pet food or a bag of birdseed, and you have shelter and food in one place.

Because people spend less time in a garage or basement than in a kitchen or bedroom, activity there can build for weeks before anyone notices droppings behind a stack of boxes or a chewed corner on a stored item.

Common trouble spots we check.

  • Garage door thresholds, especially where the rubber seal has worn down or lifted at the corners.
  • Gaps around dryer vents, garage door opener wiring, and other utility penetrations.
  • Foundation cracks and gaps where a basement or garage slab meets the framing above.
  • Stored cardboard boxes, which mice readily chew into for both shelter and nesting material.
  • Areas behind water heaters, furnaces and stored furniture where droppings collect unnoticed.

How a garage or basement visit works.

We start by checking the door thresholds, utility penetrations and any visible foundation gaps, since these are the most common entry points. If boxes or stored items are in the way, we may ask you to move a few so we can see behind them, though we try to work around what is already there where possible.

Once we know where activity is concentrated, we place traps or tamper-resistant stations directly on the routes we found, then seal the entry points with materials rodents cannot chew through. Foam and steel wool are not durable long-term fixes in a garage that sees temperature swings and moisture, so we use hardware cloth, sheet metal and sealant instead.

Protecting stored items and wiring.

Rodents chewing through stored boxes is an inconvenience, but chewed wiring in a garage, whether from a garage door opener, an appliance, or a vehicle parked inside, is a more serious concern. We check exposed wiring for gnaw marks during the inspection and flag any damage that needs an electrician's attention rather than attempting electrical repairs ourselves.

If a vehicle parked in the garage has chewed wiring or a nest in the engine bay, our vehicle and car rodent removal service covers that specifically, since a car needs a different approach than the surrounding structure.

Sealing the space once activity clears.

Once traps and stations have cleared the active animals, sealing the garage or basement entry points is what keeps the same problem from starting over. That work is covered under our rodent exclusion service. For a garage specifically it often means a new door threshold seal, hardware cloth over a vent, or mortar in a foundation crack, using materials chosen to hold up to temperature swings and moisture rather than relying on foam alone.

Basements and moisture in the Puyallup area.

Not every home in the area has a full basement, since crawl space construction is common, but where a basement does exist, ground moisture from the region's wet fall and winter can be a factor. A damp basement corner gives rodents both cover and, in some cases, a water source, which makes drainage and moisture control worth mentioning if you have had recurring activity in the same spot.

Attached garages that share a wall or ceiling with the living space deserve extra attention. A gap that seems contained to the garage side can actually give rodents a path straight into a bedroom or hallway closet on the other side of that shared wall. We check both sides of shared walls when the layout allows it, rather than assuming the garage and house are fully separate.

What helps before we arrive.

  • Move stored boxes away from walls where you have seen droppings, if you can do so safely.
  • Note whether the garage door threshold has visible gaps or wear.
  • Mention any recent vehicle issues that might point to rodents in an engine bay.
  • Keep pets away from areas with suspected activity until the visit.

Questions about garage and basement rodent control

Why does my garage seem to get mice more than the rest of my house?

Garages usually sit close to grade with more entry points, such as door thresholds and utility penetrations, and stored boxes give mice both shelter and nesting material in one place.

Should I stop storing boxes in the garage?

Switching to sealed plastic bins instead of cardboard makes a real difference, since mice cannot chew into plastic as easily and it removes an easy nesting material source.

Can rodents damage my garage door opener?

Yes, chewed wiring on a garage door opener is a fairly common finding. We check for gnaw marks on visible wiring and flag any damage for repair.

Do you check basements even if my home mostly has a crawl space?

Yes. If your home has any basement area, even a partial one, we include it in the inspection along with the crawl space and garage.

What if the smell is coming from inside a garage wall?

That usually points to a dead rodent decomposing in a void. Our dead rodent removal and odor control service locates and removes the source rather than just covering the smell.

Will sealing the garage door threshold really help?

Yes, a worn or gapped threshold is one of the most common mouse entry points we find in garages, and replacing or sealing it closes an easy route.
