What rodent damage typically looks like.

Mice and rats chew for two reasons: to gather nesting material and to keep their teeth worn down, since rodent teeth grow continuously. That means almost anything soft enough to chew becomes a target, from fiberglass insulation to ductwork seams to the paper facing on drywall. The damage is rarely limited to one small spot once an infestation has been active for a while.

In Puyallup homes, we see this most often in attic insulation, crawl space vapor barriers, and ductwork that runs through an attic or crawl space, since those are the areas rodents nest in most. Soffit vents and eave areas also take damage when rodents chew an opening larger to fit through.

Damaged materialCommon locationRepair approach

Chewed electrical wiring is a special case. We identify and photograph it clearly, but we do not repair wiring ourselves, since that work belongs with a licensed electrician. We can coordinate the timing so sealing and insulation work happens around an electrician's visit rather than before it.

Repairing damage before sealing the entry point just gives rodents something new to chew.

Why we repair after sealing, not before.

If rodents can still get into a space, repairing insulation or ductwork there is only a temporary fix, since new material is just as chewable as the old. Our rodent exclusion service closes entry points first, and repair work follows once we are confident the space is actually sealed off from new activity.

The sequence matters: trap or remove active animals, seal the entry points, then repair the damage. Skipping ahead to repair before the first two steps usually means paying for the same work twice.

Assessing the extent of damage.

During the inspection, we look at how far chewing and contamination extend, not just the most visible spot. A single chewed duct seam might be an isolated repair, while insulation soiled across a large section of an attic points to a longer-running problem that needs a bigger scope. We explain the difference clearly so you understand what you are paying to fix and why.

If droppings and urine have contaminated a wider area than just the chewed material, that falls under cleanup and sanitizing through our attic and crawl space rodent cleanup service, which we can schedule together with repair work.

Cedar siding and trim, common on homes in the Puyallup area, present their own repair consideration, since gnawed cedar boards can be harder to color-match than a painted surface. We are upfront about that limitation rather than promising a perfect match, and we can point you toward comparable materials where we can find them. Where a full board replacement is not practical right away, a temporary patch can hold until a larger siding project is planned, as long as it is checked periodically to make sure it is still keeping rodents out. The same honesty applies to attic wood and framing: a stained or lightly scarred rafter can often be left in place once cleaned, while a heavily gnawed structural piece is a matter for a carpenter rather than a cosmetic fix.

Insurance and rodent damage.

Homeowners insurance often does not cover rodent damage, since most policies treat it as a maintenance issue rather than a sudden, accidental event. We are not insurance professionals and cannot tell you how your specific policy will respond, but we can provide photos and a written description of what we found and repaired, which some homeowners use when checking with their insurer.

Working with existing repair contractors.

If you already have a contractor or handyman lined up for repair work, we are glad to coordinate rather than insist on handling every piece ourselves. Our part of the job is confirming the space is sealed and documenting what was chewed and where, so whoever does the actual carpentry, drywall or electrical repair has an accurate scope to work from instead of guessing at what needs replacing.

Repairs after a long-running problem.

If a rodent problem went unnoticed for a long stretch, perhaps in an attic or crawl space that is rarely visited, the repair scope can be larger than a homeowner expects going in. We would rather show you the full extent honestly during the inspection than discover more damage partway through the work.

Before we arrive.

  • Do not touch any wiring you suspect has been chewed.
  • Take photos of visible damage if you can safely access the area.
  • Note whether trapping or sealing has already been done on the property.
  • Mention if you plan to file an insurance claim, so we can document accordingly.

Questions about rodent damage repair

Do you repair chewed electrical wiring?

No, we identify and document wiring damage clearly, but repair work goes to a licensed electrician. We can time our work around that repair.

Will homeowners insurance cover rodent damage?

Often not, since most policies treat rodent damage as a maintenance issue rather than a covered event. Check directly with your insurer, and we can provide documentation of what we found if that helps.

Do you repair before or after sealing entry points?

After. Repairing chewed material while rodents can still get in usually means the new material gets chewed too, so we seal first through our rodent exclusion service.

How do I know if the damage is bigger than what I can see?

That is part of what the inspection is for. We check beyond the most visible spot, since insulation and ductwork damage often extends further than what is obvious from a quick look.

Can you replace crawl space vapor barrier?

Yes, torn or chewed vapor barrier is a common repair item in Puyallup crawl spaces, and we patch smaller tears or replace larger sections as needed.

What if I just want an estimate for repair, not the full service?

Our rodent inspection and estimates service covers exactly that, giving you a clear picture of scope and next steps before committing to the work.
