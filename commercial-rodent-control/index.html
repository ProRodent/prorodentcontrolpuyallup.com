Why commercial properties need a different approach.

A restaurant, warehouse or retail shop has different stakes than a house. A rodent sighting during business hours affects customers and staff, food storage areas carry health code implications, and loading docks and storage rooms create entry points a residential inspection would not encounter. Commercial work also often needs documentation for a health inspector, landlord or corporate office.

We schedule commercial visits around your operating hours where possible, and we can work discreetly in areas customers do not see, such as stockrooms and loading docks, rather than disrupting a business floor during open hours.

Businesses we work with.

  • Restaurants and food service businesses, where droppings or sightings can trigger a health code issue.
  • Warehouses and storage facilities, where large open spaces and loading docks create multiple entry points.
  • Retail shops and offices, where stockrooms and break rooms are common activity spots.
  • Properties near the Washington State Fairgrounds, where seasonal fair activity and nearby food vendors can increase pressure on surrounding businesses.

Monitoring and ongoing service.

Many commercial properties benefit from monitoring stations checked on a recurring schedule rather than a single visit, especially food service locations and warehouses near green space or a loading dock that sees regular deliveries. We set a schedule that fits the property's risk level and can adjust frequency based on what monitoring turns up over time.

If a specific event or season increases pressure, such as the Fair drawing more activity to nearby food-related businesses, we can schedule additional checks around that window rather than waiting for the next regular visit.

Sealing a commercial building.

Commercial buildings have their own set of common entry points: loading dock doors that do not seal tightly, gaps around large HVAC penetrations, and utility conduits running into a building at multiple points. Our rodent exclusion service covers this work, scaled to the size and layout of a commercial property rather than a single-family home.

Vehicles and outdoor storage on commercial lots.

Commercial properties often have fleet vehicles, outdoor storage areas or dumpster enclosures that create the same kind of pressure a residential yard would, only at a larger scale. A dumpster enclosure with a gap at the base, a pallet storage area against the back of a building, or delivery vehicles parked overnight near green space can all draw rodents toward a building even when the interior is well maintained. We look at these outdoor features as part of a full commercial inspection rather than only checking the interior.

Staff training and reporting.

Staff on the floor or in the stockroom often notice signs of activity before anyone calls us, whether that is a dropping behind a shelf or a chewed corner on a storage box. We can walk staff through a short list of what to look for and who to tell internally, so a small sign gets reported quickly instead of sitting unnoticed until it becomes a bigger problem. This is especially useful for businesses with shift changes, where the person who spots something is not always the one who calls us.

Documentation for landlords and inspectors.

If you need a record of what was found and treated, whether for your own files, a landlord, or a health inspector, we can provide a written description and photos of findings. We are not a substitute for an official health department inspection, and we say so plainly rather than implying our visit carries that authority.

For multi-unit or mixed-use commercial buildings, we coordinate with whoever manages the property, since a single business tenant often cannot authorize sealing work on shared structural areas without the landlord's involvement.

We also work with property management companies that oversee several commercial buildings across Puyallup, setting up a consistent inspection and monitoring schedule across the portfolio rather than treating each building as an unrelated one-off call. That consistency helps catch a developing problem at one property before it spreads to a neighboring unit in the same complex.

What to have ready for a commercial visit.

  • Access to stockrooms, break rooms and loading dock areas.
  • Any prior pest control records or health inspection notes, if available.
  • Preferred scheduling windows around your operating hours.
  • Contact information for the property manager or landlord if the building is not owner-occupied.

Questions about commercial rodent control

Can you work outside our normal business hours?

Yes, we schedule commercial visits around your operating hours where possible, including early morning or after-close appointments for businesses that need minimal disruption.

Do you provide documentation for a health inspection?

We can provide a written description and photos of what we found and treated, though we are not a substitute for an official health department inspection.

How often should a restaurant have monitoring stations checked?

It depends on the property and its risk factors, such as proximity to food storage, loading docks or green space. We set a recurring schedule based on what the initial inspection and early monitoring show.

Can you seal a large warehouse the same way as a house?

The materials and principles are the same, hardware cloth, sheet metal and sealant rather than foam, but the scope is scaled to loading docks, large HVAC penetrations and perimeter length specific to a bigger building.

Who do I contact if I lease my business space?

Structural sealing work often needs landlord coordination, since a tenant typically cannot authorize changes to shared building areas. We are used to working with both tenants and property managers.

Does being near the Fairgrounds increase our risk?

It can. Seasonal fair activity brings more food vendors and crowds to the area, which can temporarily increase rodent pressure on nearby businesses, especially food service ones.
