Why can't a price be given over the phone?

The scope of work depends on things that only become clear during an inspection: how many entry points exist, how extensive the activity is, whether the species is mice or rats, and whether sealing, cleanup or repair is also needed. A quote given without seeing the property would be a guess, and we would rather give you an accurate number after a look than a low estimate that changes later.

Our rodent inspection service is the starting point for any project, and the visit itself gives us what we need to put together an accurate plan and cost.

What are the main factors that affect cost?

The species matters, since rat work often requires different trap and bait placement strategies than mouse work, and rats typically need larger-scale exclusion materials given their bigger entry points. The extent of the problem matters too: a single mouse caught early is a smaller job than an established population that has been active for months across multiple rooms.

Access also plays a role. A property with a hard-to-reach crawl space or a steep roofline takes more time and sometimes different equipment than a straightforward single-story home with easy attic access.

What does a typical visit involve?

An inspection covers the exterior and interior systematically, looking for entry points, droppings, nesting material and any signs of the extent of activity. From there, we explain what we found and outline a plan, which usually includes some combination of trapping, exclusion and, if needed, cleanup or repair.

Our rodent trapping and removal service addresses animals already present, and rodent exclusion closes the entry points so new ones don't follow. Whether both are needed, or just one, depends on what the inspection finds.

Does location within our coverage area affect cost?

Distance from our Puyallup base is one factor among several, never a flat surcharge we quote over the phone. Properties farther out in more rural parts of our coverage area, with multiple outbuildings or larger acreage, may see more time spent on inspection simply due to the size of the property, not the distance traveled.

Are commercial properties priced differently than homes?

Commercial properties often involve different considerations, such as documentation for health inspections, ongoing monitoring contracts, or coordination around business hours. Our commercial rodent control service covers these needs, and pricing reflects the scope of the specific building and its use.

What about follow-up visits?

Most rodent problems benefit from at least one follow-up visit to confirm the treatment worked and the seal is holding. Whether follow-up is included in an initial plan or scheduled separately depends on the scope of the original work, and we explain that clearly before starting.

An established, months-old problem is more likely to need multiple visits than a small, early one, simply because more animals and more entry points take more time to fully resolve.

Does severity affect the number of visits needed?

A light problem caught early, with a single entry point and a small number of animals, often resolves within one or two visits: an inspection, treatment, and a follow-up to confirm the activity has stopped. A more established situation generally needs more visits spread over a longer period. Multiple entry points and a larger population that has been building for weeks or months both add time, since trapping a large population takes patience and sealing several gaps is more involved than closing just one.

This is part of why calling as soon as you notice early signs, rather than waiting to see if it goes away on its own, tends to keep the overall scope and time investment smaller. Our post on how to get rid of mice in your house covers the typical timeline for a DIY-versus-professional approach in more detail.

What role does the type of structure play?

A single-story home with straightforward attic and crawl space access is generally more efficient to fully inspect and treat than a multi-story home, a property with an unusually complex roofline, or a rural property with several separate buildings. Each additional structure, whether a detached garage, a workshop or a barn, needs its own check for entry points, which adds time to the overall visit even when the treatment itself for each structure is relatively simple.

Vehicles and equipment stored on a property can also factor into a visit if they show signs of activity. Our vehicle and car rodent removal service is a distinct piece of work from a home or structure inspection, even when both get addressed during the same overall visit to the property.

How can I get an accurate number for my situation?

Call and describe what you're seeing: where the activity is, how long it's been going on, and whether you've noticed anything specific like droppings, chewed material or a smell. We'll schedule an inspection, and that visit is what gives us the information needed to put together a real plan and cost specific to your property.

What we look at before quoting a plan.

  • The species involved and how extensive the activity appears to be.
  • Every entry point we can identify during the inspection.
  • Whether cleanup or repair is needed alongside trapping and sealing.
  • How many structures on the property need attention.
  • Your timeline and any urgency involved.

Common questions

Can you give me a ballpark number over the phone?

We can discuss the general factors that affect cost, but an accurate number requires an inspection, since scope varies a lot by property, species and how long the activity has been going on.

Is the inspection itself a separate cost from the treatment?

The inspection is the starting point for any plan, and we'll explain the full scope and total cost clearly before any treatment work actually begins.

Do rural properties with barns or outbuildings cost more?

Properties with multiple structures often take more time to inspect fully, since each building needs its own check for entry points.

Does emergency or same-day service cost more?

We don't add flat emergency surcharges. Cost depends on the scope of work needed, which is assessed the same way whether the visit happens today or next week.

Do you offer any kind of ongoing monitoring plan?

For commercial properties especially, ongoing monitoring is something we can discuss as part of a commercial rodent control plan built around the property's specific layout and traffic patterns.

Do you charge extra for larger properties with multiple buildings?

Cost reflects the actual scope of work, including how many structures need inspection, rather than a flat per-building fee. We explain the plan and cost clearly before starting any work on the property.
