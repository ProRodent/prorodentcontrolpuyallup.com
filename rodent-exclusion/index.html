Why sealing matters as much as removal.

Removing the rodents that are inside a house solves today's problem. It does not answer the question of how they got in, and if that gap stays open, new mice or rats eventually find the same scent trail. Exclusion work closes that gap, which is what actually stops the cycle rather than just resetting the clock every few months.

A mouse needs a gap of only about 1/4 inch to squeeze through, and a rat can fit through roughly 1/2 inch. That is smaller than most homeowners expect, which is why common trouble spots like utility penetrations, vent screens and door thresholds get overlooked until we point them out during an inspection.

Common entry points on Puyallup homes.

  • Crawl space vents with torn or missing screens, since most homes in the area sit over a crawl space.
  • Garage door thresholds worn down enough to show daylight underneath.
  • Gaps where plumbing, electrical or gas lines pass through exterior walls or the foundation.
  • Roofline and eave gaps, especially where a chimney, dormer or addition meets the main roof.
  • Gaps around cedar siding and trim where boards have shifted or warped from moisture over time.

We do not rely on foam or loose steel wool as a long-term fix. Foam degrades and rodents can chew through it, and steel wool rusts and loosens over time in a wet climate, which lets a sealed gap reopen within a year or two.

A gap sealed with the wrong material is just a delay, not a fix.

How an exclusion visit works.

We walk the full exterior first, marking every gap of concern rather than sealing the first one we find and calling it done. That includes checking the roofline from ground level and, where needed, from a ladder, along with the crawl space vents, garage thresholds and any visible foundation cracks.

Once we have the full list, we seal in an order that makes sense, usually starting with active entry points identified during trapping or treatment, then moving to secondary gaps that are not yet in use but could become one. If your property has already had trapping or treatment through our rodent trapping and removal service, we coordinate the sealing to happen once activity is confirmed under control.

Sealing after damage is repaired.

If rodents have already chewed through insulation, ductwork or drywall, sealing the entry point is only half the job. Our rodent damage repair service covers replacing or repairing what was damaged, and we often schedule that work right after sealing so the same crawl space or attic access is not opened twice.

Sealing before the wet season.

Western Washington's long, wet fall and winter are when rodents push hardest toward dry shelter, which makes late summer and early fall the best window to seal a home before pressure increases. If you have had activity in past winters, sealing now rather than waiting for the first signs of a new season is the more effective order of operations.

That said, we seal homes year-round, and an active problem in the middle of winter still gets the same inspection and sealing approach. The timing changes how urgent the work is, not what we do.

Cedar siding and older trim.

Cedar siding and trim, common across the Puyallup area, moves with moisture over the seasons, and boards that have warped or shrunk slightly can open small gaps that were not there when the house was built. We check siding seams and trim joints as part of the exterior survey, since these gaps are easy to miss from a distance but can be exactly the size a mouse needs.

What sealing does and does not fix.

No sealing job makes a house permanently immune to rodents, since materials shift, new gaps can open from settling or storm damage, and a home surrounded by heavy brush or a rodent-friendly neighbor's yard still faces outside pressure. What sealing does is close the specific gaps we find during the inspection and remove the easiest routes in, which meaningfully reduces future activity even though no sealing job can promise a house will never see another rodent.

What helps before we arrive.

  • Clear access to the crawl space entry, attic hatch and garage corners.
  • Note any areas where you have seen daylight, drafts or gaps from inside.
  • Mention any recent additions, remodels or storm damage that may have created new gaps.
  • Let us know if trapping or treatment has already been done, or is planned separately.

Questions about rodent exclusion and entry point sealing

Will sealing alone get rid of rodents already inside?

No. Sealing closes future entry points, but rodents already inside need to be trapped or treated first. We usually coordinate the two so nothing is sealed while animals are still trapped inside.

Do you use poison as part of sealing?

Sealing itself does not involve bait. If treatment is also needed, we use tamper-resistant stations rather than loose poison, and we explain that separately from the sealing scope.

How long does sealing last?

Materials like hardware cloth, sheet metal and mortar hold up well over years, though normal home settling, storm damage or a remodel can create new gaps over time. We do not promise the seal will never be tested again, since conditions on a property can change.

Is my crawl space really a common entry point?

Yes. Most homes in the Puyallup area sit over a crawl space, and torn vent screens or a poorly sealed access door are some of the most common gaps we find.

Can you seal a house that already has chewed wiring or insulation?

Yes, though we usually pair sealing with our rodent damage repair service so the damaged material is replaced around the same time the entry point is closed.

Do you seal commercial buildings too?

Yes. Our commercial rodent control service includes exclusion work for shops, offices and warehouses, which often have different entry points than a house.
