Auburn sits on the county line.

Auburn is its own incorporated city, with the bulk of the community in King County and a southern portion extending into Pierce County. That split does not change how we handle a call, since our coverage is based on the 98001 and 98092 ZIPs rather than which county side an address happens to sit on. Tell us your ZIP and address when you call and we will confirm coverage either way.

The city sits in the Green River valley, and low-lying ground near the river and its tributaries shares some of the same conditions we see near the Puyallup River, including damp soil and drainage corridors that draw rats toward nearby structures.

What we handle on calls from Auburn.

Calls from Auburn addresses often describe rat activity near a ditch, greenbelt or fence line, mice moving into a garage or crawl space as the weather turns wet, or a smell suggesting a dead rodent in a wall. Our rat control service addresses the outdoor, ground-level pattern common near the valley floor, while mouse control covers the indoor side of the same seasonal shift.

Sealing homes across Auburn's neighborhoods.

Auburn includes both older in-town housing stock near downtown and newer development on the surrounding hillsides, and each type of construction tends to develop its own gaps over time: settled sills and additions in older homes, and unsealed utility penetrations in newer ones. Our rodent exclusion service closes those gaps with hardware cloth, sheet metal and mortar rather than foam, which holds up poorly through a wet Pacific Northwest winter.

If chewed insulation or wiring has already resulted from an earlier rodent problem, our rodent damage repair service covers putting that material right once the active animals are cleared and the entry point is sealed.

Why the shed is worth checking before the house.

On a lot that backs onto the Green River or a connected slough, we've learned it's usually faster to start at the shed than the house itself. A gap under a shed floor sits closer to whatever's drawing rats in from the water in the first place, so it tends to show activity before the main structure does. Ruling that out first, rather than assuming the house is where the problem began, saves a callback more often than not.
