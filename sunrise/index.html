Why Sunrise needs its own approach

  • Established landscaping

Decades-old landscaping and rodent cover.

Sunrise includes streets where landscaping has had many years to mature, and dense, overgrown shrubs against a foundation are one of the most reliable ways to draw rodents close to a house without anyone noticing until droppings or a burrow show up. Established hedges along property lines also give rats a protected corridor between yards, similar to a bramble patch but often overlooked since a trimmed hedge looks tidy from a distance even when the base is thick and hollow.

If your property has landscaping that has grown untouched at the base for years, checking the soil line where shrubs meet the foundation is worth doing before calling. Fresh burrow holes or a worn path through the mulch are visible signs even before an inspection.

A mix of housing ages, block by block.

Unlike a subdivision built all at once, Sunrise has a mix of older homes and newer infill construction on the same streets, which means the common entry points vary more than in a uniformly aged neighborhood. An older home nearby may have settled foundation gaps, while a newer one on the same block might have an unsealed utility penetration instead. We do not assume a fixed pattern here and inspect each property on its own condition rather than by the age of the surrounding block.

If you know your home's approximate build year, mentioning it when you call gives us a useful starting point, though the inspection itself is what actually determines where to look.

Pressure from nearby arterial roads.

Streets closer to busier arterial roads sometimes see rodents pushed off commercial strips, gas stations or green median strips toward quieter residential blocks, particularly as construction or new development disturbs an existing population nearby. If a new commercial project or road work has started near your street, that disruption is a reasonable factor to mention on the call, since it can explain a sudden change in activity that was not there before.

Garages and sheds in Sunrise yards.

Detached garages and sheds are common on Sunrise's larger, older lots, and these structures often get less regular attention than the main house. A shed used for seasonal storage, garden tools or overflow pantry items is an easy target, and our backyard and outdoor rodent control service covers that ground-level structure work directly, including sheds set apart from the home.

Dead animal smells in older Sunrise homes.

Sunrise's mix of older homes sometimes turns up a dead-animal smell in a wall or crawl space that has gone unnoticed for a while, particularly in a house with limited attic or crawl access. Our dead rodent removal and odor control service handles locating and removing the source rather than leaving a homeowner to guess at which wall is responsible.

Older detached garages and irregular gaps.

Some of Sunrise's older detached garages were built before current sealing standards existed, and doors, siding and foundation edges on these structures can have irregular gaps that do not match the tidy, predictable sizes found on newer construction. Our rodent exclusion service adapts sealing materials to whatever gap shape is actually there rather than assuming a standard fix will fit. Older garages sometimes need a custom-cut piece of hardware cloth or flashing rather than an off-the-shelf part.

What we hear from Sunrise callers.

Calls from this area often describe activity near mature hedges or shrubs, a shed that has not been checked in years, or new activity coinciding with nearby construction or road work. As with every part of Puyallup, we confirm the species and entry point during the inspection rather than assuming based on the neighborhood alone, then move through treatment and sealing from there.

Questions about Sunrise

Does mature landscaping really increase rodent risk?

Yes, dense, overgrown shrubs and hedges against a foundation give rodents cover and a hidden approach to any gap in the siding, and thick hedges along property lines can act as a travel corridor between yards.

My street has a mix of old and new homes. Does that matter?

It means entry points are less predictable block to block, so we base the inspection on your specific property's condition rather than assuming a single pattern for the area.

Could nearby road construction be causing new rodent activity?

It is possible. Construction or new development can disturb an existing rodent population and push it toward nearby residential properties, so mention any recent nearby work when you call.

Do you check detached sheds as part of a Sunrise visit?

Yes, sheds and detached garages are included, and we check them the same way as the main house for entry points and signs of activity.

Should I trim back my hedges to reduce rodent risk?

Trimming the base of hedges and shrubs away from the foundation reduces the hidden cover rodents use, and it is a reasonable step even without a confirmed problem yet.
