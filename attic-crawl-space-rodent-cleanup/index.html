Why these spaces need a separate cleanup step.

Attics and crawl spaces are out of sight, which means rodent activity there often goes unnoticed far longer than activity in a kitchen or garage. By the time a homeowner hears scratching overhead or notices an odor from below the floor, droppings, urine and nesting material may have built up over weeks or months, not just a few days.

Cleanup is a distinct step from trapping and sealing, because contaminated insulation and droppings left in place can hold odor and health concerns long after the live animals are gone. We treat cleanup as its own phase of the job rather than an afterthought.

What we find in Puyallup attics and crawl spaces.

  • Droppings concentrated near nesting sites, often along the edges where insulation meets framing.
  • Urine-soaked insulation, which loses its effectiveness and can carry odor into the living space.
  • Chewed wiring, ductwork or vapor barrier, especially in a crawl space where a vapor barrier keeps ground moisture out.
  • Nesting material such as shredded insulation, paper or fabric packed into corners or wall voids.

We do not sweep or vacuum dry droppings, since that can send contaminated dust into the air. The CDC's guidance on cleaning up after rodents calls for ventilating the space, wearing gloves, and wetting droppings with disinfectant before removal, and we follow that approach on every cleanup.

Cleanup only happens after the animals are actually gone, not alongside active trapping.

Cleanup after trapping and sealing.

We schedule cleanup once trapping through our rodent trapping and removal service has confirmed the space is clear, and often alongside sealing work from our rodent exclusion service so the same access point is not opened more than necessary. Doing cleanup before the animals are fully removed risks re-contaminating a space we just cleaned.

If chewed wiring, ductwork or structural material needs replacing rather than just cleaning, our rodent damage repair service covers that separately, since repair work is a different scope than sanitizing.

Crawl spaces and Puyallup's wet climate.

A crawl space in western Washington deals with more ground moisture than one in a drier region, and a torn or missing vapor barrier lets that moisture rise into the space. Rodents nesting in a crawl space can tear the vapor barrier further while building nests, which compounds a moisture problem that was already a concern before any rodents arrived. We check the vapor barrier condition as part of the cleanup, not as a separate add-on.

Attics with roof rat history.

Attics that have hosted roof rats tend to show a different pattern than a mouse problem. Droppings are fewer and larger, concentrated near one or two nest sites rather than scattered widely, and chewing shows up more on structural wood and roof sheathing since roof rats favor higher, more sheltered nooks. If your attic access is difficult to reach, such as a small hatch with no permanent ladder, tell us that when you call so we can bring the right equipment rather than discovering the access problem on site.

Ventilation and drying afterward.

After contaminated material is removed, we leave the space ventilated where possible so any residual dampness or odor can clear rather than getting trapped in a closed-up attic or crawl space. In a crawl space, this can mean confirming vent screens are back in place and functional once the sealing work is done, so airflow keeps moving instead of turning the space stagnant again.

What to expect during a cleanup visit.

A technician suits up with the right protective gear before entering the attic or crawl space, since droppings and urine residue require careful handling. We remove contaminated material, disinfect surfaces, and replace insulation where it has been soiled beyond saving. We photograph significant damage so you can see what was found and what was done.

Once cleanup is finished, we walk through what we found and what, if anything, needs a follow-up visit, whether that is a repair item or a spot we want to recheck for new activity.

Before we arrive.

  • Do not enter the attic or crawl space yourself if you suspect heavy droppings or a dead animal.
  • Keep pets and children away from the attic hatch or crawl space access.
  • Note any smell, and which part of the house it is strongest in.
  • Clear a path to the access point if you can do so without disturbing the area.

Questions about attic and crawl space rodent cleanup

Is it safe to clean up droppings myself?

You can, but public health guidance recommends ventilating the space first, wearing gloves, and wetting droppings with disinfectant rather than sweeping or vacuuming them dry. If the area is large or you are unsure, it is safer to have it done professionally.

Do you replace insulation after cleanup?

Yes, when insulation has been soiled by urine or droppings badly enough that it has lost its effectiveness or holds a lasting odor, we remove and replace it.

How do I know if my crawl space vapor barrier is damaged?

Torn plastic sheeting on the ground, visible soil, or a persistent musty smell from below the floor are signs worth checking. We inspect the vapor barrier condition as part of a crawl space cleanup.

Do you do cleanup without trapping first?

We prefer to confirm the space is clear of active rodents before cleanup, since cleaning an area that still has animals in it usually means redoing the work. If you only need cleanup after handling trapping elsewhere, tell us that when you call.

Can chewed wiring in the attic really be a fire risk?

Yes, damaged wiring insulation is a recognized fire hazard. We do not repair electrical work ourselves, but we flag it clearly so you can have it addressed.

How long does a cleanup visit take?

It depends on the size of the space and how much material needs removal. A light cleanup can take under two hours, while a heavily soiled attic or crawl space can take longer.
