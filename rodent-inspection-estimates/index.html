Why the inspection comes first.

A rodent problem is really two separate questions: what animal is active, and where is it getting in. Guessing at either one wastes time and money, since a trap in the wrong spot catches nothing and a sealed gap that was never the entry point does not stop the activity. The inspection answers both questions before any treatment plan is set.

In Puyallup, that usually means checking the crawl space access, garage thresholds, roofline vents and any gaps where utility lines pass through siding, along with the yard if the signs point outdoors. Older homes downtown and newer construction on South Hill tend to have different weak points, and a technician who has seen both knows where to look first.

What we check during an inspection.

  • Exterior foundation, siding and roofline for gaps, gnaw marks and grease trails.
  • Crawl space vents and access doors, since that space sits directly under most Puyallup homes.
  • Garage thresholds, utility penetrations and any storage areas where droppings or nesting material collect.
  • Attic insulation and framing for nesting, chewed wiring and droppings if the signs point upward.
  • Yard features such as sheds, woodpiles, brambles and drainage ditches that give rodents cover close to the house.

How the estimate is built.

Once the inspection is done, we explain what we found and what it will take to fix it. That includes which service or services apply, such as mouse control, rat control or rodent exclusion, and roughly how many visits the plan is likely to need. We would rather explain the reasoning than hand you a number with no context.

If the inspection turns up more than one issue, such as active mice plus chewed insulation from an earlier rat problem, we walk through each piece separately so you can see what is urgent and what can be scheduled next. Our rodent damage repair page covers what happens when chewed material needs to be replaced rather than just cleaned.

A plan built on what we actually found works better than one built on a guess.

Crawl space and roofline access.

A thorough inspection sometimes means getting into spaces a homeowner would rather not deal with personally, such as a tight crawl space entry or a roofline that needs a ladder to see clearly. We bring the equipment needed to check those areas properly rather than skipping them because they are hard to reach, since an overlooked crawl space vent or roofline gap is exactly the kind of thing that gets missed in a rushed inspection.

Inspections for buyers, sellers and renters.

We get calls from people buying or selling a home in the Puyallup area who want a rodent check before closing, and from renters who want documentation of activity before or after a lease. We can inspect and describe what we find in plain terms, though we do not provide the kind of formal pest inspection report some lenders require for a specific loan program. Ask when you call if you need something for a real estate transaction, and we will tell you honestly whether our visit fits that need.

What to expect during the visit.

Expect the technician to spend real time looking before touching anything. That can mean getting into a crawl space, checking behind stored items in a garage, or walking the yard perimeter. You do not need to clean the whole house first, but clearing access to the crawl space entry and any storage areas near walls helps the inspection move faster.

After the walk-through, we talk through what we found, answer questions, and give you the estimate before starting any treatment. There is no pressure to book everything at once if you want to think it over, though we will tell you plainly if a delay is likely to let the problem grow.

We also inspect properties that have never had a reported rodent problem, for homeowners who simply want a baseline check before symptoms show up, particularly ahead of the wetter months when activity typically increases. A preventive inspection can catch a developing gap before it turns into an active infestation, which is usually a smaller and less disruptive fix.

Have this ready for a faster visit.

  • A rough timeline of when you first noticed signs.
  • Access to the crawl space, attic or garage areas where you saw activity.
  • Any photos of droppings, gnaw marks or dead animals you have already taken.
  • Whether pets or children use the areas where activity was seen.

Questions about rodent inspection and estimates

How much does an inspection cost?

Pricing depends on the property and what the visit involves. Call and ask directly so you have a clear answer before booking.

How long does an inspection take?

Most inspections take under an hour, though a larger property or a harder-to-reach crawl space can take longer. We would rather be thorough than fast.

Do you inspect crawl spaces?

Yes. Most homes in the Puyallup area sit over a crawl space, and checking the vents and access door is one of the first things we do.

Can you inspect before I buy or rent a house?

Yes, we can walk a property and tell you what we see, though we do not produce the specific formal report some lenders require. Ask when you call if that distinction matters for your situation.

What happens if you don't find any current activity?

We tell you that directly. Sometimes an inspection turns up old droppings from a resolved problem rather than anything active, and in that case we talk about prevention rather than treatment.

Do I need to be home for the inspection?

For most of the exterior work, no, but we prefer someone be present to open the crawl space or garage access and to answer questions about what you have noticed.
