Trapping is a placement problem, not a product problem.

A snap trap or bait station only works if it sits on a route the animal actually uses. Placing traps randomly around a room, or lining a garage wall without checking for grease marks first, catches far fewer rodents than placing the same trap two feet to one side, right on the runway. Most of the skill in trapping is in the placement, not the trap itself.

That is why we start with a short inspection before setting anything. Droppings, gnaw marks and grease trails tell us where mice or rats are actually moving, whether that is along a garage wall, behind a water heater, or at the crawl space access. Traps go on those routes, not on a guess.

Traps and stations we use.

  • Snap traps for mice, placed perpendicular to walls where droppings and rub marks concentrate.
  • Larger snap traps or multi-catch options for heavier rat activity along foundations and fence lines.
  • Tamper-resistant bait stations where the site allows it, placed out of reach of children, pets and wildlife.
  • Live-capture options in specific situations where they fit the property better than a lethal trap.

We do not scatter loose bait or poison, since it puts pets and wildlife at risk and can leave a dead animal decomposing somewhere hard to reach. If you already have a smell you suspect is a dead rodent, our dead rodent removal and odor control service handles that directly.

Why trapping alone is not the whole fix.

Trapping removes the animals that are inside right now. It does not close the gap they used to get in, which means new rodents can follow the same scent trail through the same opening. That is the most common reason a homeowner tells us a problem seemed to go away, then came back within a season.

For a lasting fix, trapping is usually paired with sealing work through our rodent exclusion service. We can trap first to knock down active numbers, then seal once the immediate activity is under control, or do both in the same visit depending on what the inspection shows.

Follow-up and what happens to the trapped animals.

We schedule a follow-up visit to check traps and stations, remove any catches, and confirm whether activity has stopped. If catches are still happening, we reassess placement rather than just leaving the same traps out indefinitely. Live-caught animals are handled according to state guidance rather than relocated to another property, since relocation is not an effective long-term fix and can create problems elsewhere.

If the trapping turns up an unusually large number of animals or damage that looks older than the current activity, we will tell you honestly that the problem has likely been going on longer than recently noticed, and adjust the plan accordingly.

Trapping in outdoor and shared spaces.

Outdoor trapping around a Puyallup property has its own considerations, since a yard may see foot traffic from kids, pets, or neighbors, and weather affects how quickly a station needs checking. We place outdoor stations away from paths people actually walk. In a shared-fence situation, we tell you if we think a neighboring yard is likely contributing to the activity, since sealing your side of a fence line does not stop a source that starts next door.

Trapping around pets, children and food areas.

Tell us about any pets, small children or food storage areas when you call, since it changes where we are willing to place a trap or station. We can plan around a dog that has access to the garage, a cat that roams the yard, or a kitchen where kids play on the floor. If a placement does not feel right for your household, say so during the visit and we will adjust it.

Some properties need a mix of trap types in the same visit, such as snap traps inside a garage alongside ground-level stations along an outdoor fence line. We do not standardize on one trap type for the whole property when the inspection shows different kinds of activity in different areas.

Before we arrive.

  • Keep pets away from areas where you suspect activity until the visit.
  • Note where you have seen droppings, gnaw marks or grease trails.
  • Do not set your own poison or loose bait before we arrive.
  • Clear a path to the garage wall, crawl space access or attic entry if you can do so safely.

Questions about rodent trapping and removal

How many traps will you set?

It depends on the inspection findings, not a fixed number. We would rather place a few traps correctly than scatter many traps without a reason for each location.

Are the traps safe around my dog or cat?

Tell us about any pets when you call. We use tamper-resistant stations and place traps where pets cannot easily reach them, and we can adjust placement based on where your animals go.

How often do you check the traps?

We schedule a follow-up visit to check catches and activity. If you notice a full trap before that visit and want it cleared sooner, call the line and we will work you into the schedule.

What do you do with rats or mice you catch?

Live-caught animals are handled according to state guidance rather than relocated, since relocating rodents does not solve the underlying problem and can create issues elsewhere.

Will trapping alone stop the problem for good?

Trapping removes the current animals, but new ones can move in through the same gap. Pairing trapping with sealing through our rodent exclusion service gives a more lasting result.

Can you trap in a crawl space?

Yes. Crawl spaces are common access points under Puyallup homes, and we place traps and stations there when the inspection shows activity in that area.
